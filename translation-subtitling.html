Translation informed by newsroom practice

Iman Keyhanfard provides Persian-to-English and English-to-Persian translation informed by nearly two decades of English-language journalism and newsroom production. In media work, a translation must do more than read smoothly: it must preserve meaning, context, names, titles, figures, attribution and the speaker’s intent.

Assignments can range from a short statement or interview extract to a longer report, transcript or documentary script. The intended audience, publication format, terminology and deadline are confirmed before work begins.

Translation and language services

  • News and current affairs: reports, official statements, press releases, political developments, background material and editorial copy.
  • Interview and documentary material: questions, answers, interview transcripts, documentary scripts, narration scripts and recorded statements.
  • Subtitles and captions: Persian and English subtitle translation, on-screen captions, speaker labels and time-coded text for interviews and video reports.
  • Audio and video transcription: Persian or English speech transcription, interview transcripts, speaker identification and timestamps where requested and supported by the recording.
  • Proofreading and editorial review: grammar, clarity, consistency, terminology, transliteration and style checks.
  • Bilingual adaptation: adapting wording for the target audience and format without changing the source meaning or editorial position.

How translation assignments are handled

  1. Brief and source: confirm source and target languages, subject, audience, format, deadline and reference material.
  2. Translation: preserve meaning, speaker intent, names, titles, numbers, dates and attribution.
  3. Editorial review: check terminology and consistency; flag ambiguous wording, unclear audio or uncertain names instead of guessing.
  4. Delivery: provide the agreed file, such as an editable document, clean transcript, time-coded text, subtitle file or caption copy.

For subtitle work, the required file format, timecodes, reading-speed or character requirements, speaker labels and whether synchronization is included should be agreed in advance. Subtitle translation and technical subtitle timing are related but distinct tasks; the agreed scope should make clear which is required.

Quality and editorial checks

  • Consistent spelling of names and transliteration throughout a document
  • Accurate titles, dates, figures, quotations and speaker attribution
  • Clear separation between a speaker’s claim and independently established fact
  • Preservation of tone and register without adding unsupported context
  • Visible notes for unclear source passages or competing interpretations

Where the source is incomplete, inaudible or ambiguous, the uncertainty is identified. A translator should not silently invent missing words or resolve factual disputes that require editorial verification.

What to include in an inquiry
