Translation approach

Contracts often depend on small distinctions in wording. Defined terms, repeated expressions, dates, amounts, party names, references and internal cross-links need to remain consistent throughout the document. The translation process therefore prioritizes completeness and consistency rather than stylistic rewriting.

  • Maintain consistent translations for defined terms and recurring clauses.
  • Preserve numbering, headings, tables and cross-references where practical.
  • Check party names, dates, amounts, percentages and other figures against the source.
  • Flag unclear or contradictory source wording for the client’s attention.
  • Do not silently add, remove or reinterpret contractual obligations.

Possible deliverables

  • An editable translated document
  • A bilingual version for side-by-side comparison
  • A terminology list for recurring or project-specific expressions
  • A translator’s query list identifying ambiguous source passages

File format, formatting requirements, whether tables and appendices are included, revision scope and deadline are agreed in advance. For lengthy or complex documents, a short sample section can be used to confirm terminology and style before the full assignment proceeds.

Quality control and confidentiality

Before delivery, the translated text is checked for omissions, consistency of defined terms, names, dates, figures and document structure. The client should identify any approved bilingual glossary, preferred terminology or prior version that must be followed.

Only share documents through a channel appropriate to their sensitivity. Confidentiality arrangements, retention or deletion expectations and any special handling requirements should be discussed before sending sensitive material.

Important professional boundary

This is a language service only. It is not legal advice, legal interpretation, legal representation, certified or sworn translation, notarization, or a legal opinion on validity or enforceability. If a document requires legal review, official certification or acceptance by a court or authority, the client must arrange that separately with a qualified lawyer or authorized translator.
