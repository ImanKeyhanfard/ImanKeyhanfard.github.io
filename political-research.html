Research and editorial background support on Iran, the Middle East and related international developments — prepared for newsrooms, journalists, producers and organizations that need clear context and traceable sources.

Context before conclusions

Drawing on nearly two decades of English-language newsroom and editorial work, Iman Keyhanfard supports research assignments that require regional context, careful source handling and concise communication. The aim is not to replace an editor’s judgment or present opinion as fact; it is to give the commissioning team a structured basis for understanding an issue, preparing questions and checking claims.

Research can be delivered as a focused background note, a developing- story timeline, a source pack, an interview brief or a recurring monitoring summary, depending on the assignment.

Research services

  • Country and issue background: political institutions, key actors, relevant history and the context needed to understand a current development.
  • Regional monitoring: tracking significant political, diplomatic, security and economic developments across Iran and the wider Middle East.
  • Source review and claim checks: tracing statements to original documents or named sources, comparing accounts and identifying what remains unverified.
  • Chronologies and timelines: organizing events, statements and policy developments in date order, with source references.
  • Interview preparation: guest background, suggested lines of questioning, issue summaries and follow-up questions.
  • Editorial background briefs: concise notes for presenters, correspondents, producers and editors ahead of a live segment, interview or package.
  • Farsi–English source support: locating and interpreting Persian-language material and explaining relevant terminology for English-language editorial use.

Research method and source discipline

  1. Define the question, geographic scope, time period and intended editorial use.
  2. Identify primary sources where available, including official statements, documents, transcripts and direct records.
  3. Cross-check significant claims against independent reporting and other relevant sources.
  4. Separate confirmed facts, attributed claims, analysis and unresolved questions.
  5. Provide links or source details so the commissioning team can review the evidence.

Where sources conflict, the disagreement is made visible. Where a claim cannot be independently confirmed, it is labeled accordingly. Research does not guarantee that every claim can be verified, particularly in fast-moving or information-restricted situations.

Scope and professional distinction

This is editorial and open-source research support, not legal advice, intelligence collection, a security assessment, or a guarantee of predictive accuracy. Any analytical judgment is identified as analysis and supported by the evidence available at the time.
